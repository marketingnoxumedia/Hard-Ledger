# The Little Hive — Reel Build

The Little Hive is a parenting account for ages 0–3, run by Noxu Media Group. This skill takes a script that is already written and approved and turns it into shots, text, motion and a delivery package.

**The script is fixed. Do not rewrite it, tighten it, reorder it, or "improve" a line.** The user has already been through that round. Every word that appears on screen or is spoken comes from the script verbatim. If a line genuinely cannot be illustrated, say so in the shot list and propose a visual solution — never a wording change.

## Before anything else: is there a script?

The entire package derives from the script. Without it there is no age read, no emotional temperature, no beat timings, no verbatim text — so inventing one and building on top produces work that is confidently wrong and has to be thrown away.

If the script is missing or the input field came through empty, stop and ask for it. Do not fill the gap with a plausible script. While asking, it is useful to restate the parts that are script-independent and already fixed — export spec, brand application, house voice — so those aren't relitigated when the script lands. Ask at the same time whether there is a separate cover-frame hook line, and whether an existing TTS voice should be matched.

If a runtime isn't given, assume 20–35 seconds.

## Read the script first

Before writing any section, work out two things from the script alone:

- **the child's age or stage**, as the script implies it
- **the emotional temperature** — delight, reassurance, practical, or calm warning

State both in one line at the top of the output. Every visual decision follows from them: they determine which hazards to screen footage for, how old the on-screen child may be, how fast the cuts run, and how the voice reads. If the script is genuinely ambiguous on either, say so and name the reading you chose rather than quietly picking one.

## Footage source

All visuals are licensed stock from Pexels and Envato — a deliberate mix of motion clips and stills. No AI-generated footage unless a beat marks a gap that stock genuinely cannot fill, and then only as a flagged exception with a reason.

- Aim for roughly **two-thirds motion, one-third stills**. Stills carry the quieter beats and cost nothing in pacing when given a slow push-in or drift.
- **Search Envato first for anything showing an identifiable child**, especially alongside a health, feeding, sleep or safety claim — its licensing carries model releases, which is what makes a claim-adjacent child safe to publish. Use Pexels for texture, hands, objects, rooms, light and general b-roll.
- Prefer **natively vertical** assets. Where only 16:9 exists it may be cropped, but only if the subject sits comfortably in a centre 9:16 slice with no important action lost. Never letterbox. Never pillarbox with a blurred copy of the footage behind it.
- **Continuity matters more than perfection**: the same apparent child, home and light across a single reel. Say so explicitly in the shot list where clips must come from one shoot or one contributor's set — that instruction is what keeps a mixed-source reel from reading as a montage of strangers.

## Brand & visual system — identity 1c "Honeycomb Frame"

Palette, and only these:

| Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Forest Green | `#2F5E3B` | logo stroke, wordmark, text on light |
| Honey Yellow | `#F4B400` | accent only — never a full background, never used to signal alarm |
| Sky Blue | `#C8DDF2` | background chips, lower thirds, title cards |
| Warm Cream | `#FFF7E8` | base background, text on green |
| Bee Black | `#2B2B2B` | strokes and body copy |

**Logo**: hand-drawn single honeycomb cell in forest green, one stroke, warm cream fill, simplified bee inside. Clear space on all sides equals the height of the bee's head. Never below 16px. Never recolored, rotated, or placed on Honey Yellow.

**Look**: hand-drawn warmth, natural light, real homes, lived-in surfaces. Cream and daylight, not clinical white. No cold blue grade, no stock-model gloss, and no red anywhere — red reads as alarm and contradicts the safety rule. Grade selected clips toward the palette so a mixed-source reel still reads as one piece.

## Visual rules

These are the rules that protect the account's credibility with parents. Rule 2 and rule 3 are the ones that get broken by a beautiful clip.

1. **The emotional temperature sets the visual temperature**, not just the words. Delight → movement, faces, discovery, light. Reassurance → calm, close, still, soft. Practical → hands, objects, clear demonstration. Calm warning → steady and plain; never dramatize the hazard, never stage a near-miss, never use a clip of a distressed child.

2. **Every frame is modeling behavior, and stock is where this breaks.** Reject any clip or still showing unsafe practice, however pretty — loose bedding, bumpers or soft toys in a crib, an unbuckled or wrongly-installed car seat, a propped bottle, small parts within reach of an infant, a baby unattended on a raised surface. Which hazards apply depends on the age read off the script. **Check the background of every asset, not just the subject** — the crib in the corner of an otherwise perfect shot is the one that gets screenshotted. This rule outranks how good the shot looks.

3. **Cast to the age.** A script about a six-month-old must not be illustrated with a toddler; the mismatch is the fastest way to lose a parent's trust, because a parent of a six-month-old knows exactly what six months looks like. Stock is routinely mislabelled on age — judge by what is visible in the frame (head control, sitting, crawling, gait, teeth, hand use), not by the keyword that surfaced it.

4. **Show ordinary homes and ordinary families.** Vary family shape, age and skin tone across the series rather than within a single forced frame. Skip the glossy stock register — white loft, styled nursery, laughing family in coordinated linen. The parent should recognize their own living room.

5. **No comparison imagery.** Never put two children side by side in a way that invites scoring. Never show a chart, checklist, red X, or progress bar against a child.

6. **Joy needs no justification.** If the script exists for delight, let a shot simply be the delightful thing, held long enough to land.

## Format

Canvas **1080 × 1920, 30fps, 20–35 seconds**.

Safe zones — keep all text, logo and key action outside these:

- top **250px** — profile name and UI
- bottom **600px** — caption, audio strip, action buttons
- right **200px** — like/comment/share rail

Live text area: roughly **y 350 → y 1300**, full width minus the right rail. Hook text sits high in that band (**y 400–800**). Subject centered with headroom.

## Output

One line naming the age and emotional temperature, then exactly these sections, nothing else. No preamble, no summary at the end.

### A. COVER FRAME

The still that sits on the grid and stops the scroll. Describe it, give its search terms and source, and place the hook text within the live band. It must read at thumbnail size with the audio off — which means one idea, large type, and a subject that survives being shrunk to a grid tile.

### B. SHOT LIST

One entry per script beat, mapped to the script's timecodes. For each:

- **duration**
- **still or motion**
- **SOURCE** — Pexels or Envato, with the reason where the choice is licence-driven
- **SEARCH TERMS** — three to five queries likely to surface the shot, plus one fallback query for when the first set comes back empty. Write queries the way a stock library indexes footage, not the way the script is worded.
- **SELECTION CRITERIA** — what makes a returned asset a yes or a no: age of child, framing, light, and what must not be in the background. Be specific enough that someone else could shop the shot without asking.
- **framing** — wide / mid / close, subject placement in the vertical frame, and any crop needed to reach 9:16
- **motion applied** — the camera move in the clip, or the push-in / drift given to a still

Flag any beat where stock is unlikely to deliver, and say what to do instead.

### C. ON-SCREEN TEXT

Text verbatim from the script — do not reword it. For each line: the timecode it appears, position in the live band, color pairing, and entry behavior. **Two lines maximum on screen at once.** Burned-in captions across the full runtime are mandatory; most parents watch on mute with a baby nearby, so a reel that needs sound is a reel that doesn't land.

### D. MOTION & PACING

Cut rhythm against the temperature — quicker and brighter for delight, longer holds for reassurance. Alternate stills and motion so the stills read as deliberate beats rather than gaps. Transitions stay simple: no whip pans, no glitch, no countdown timers, no arrows pointing at a child. Note where a beat should breathe.

### E. BRAND APPLICATION

Logo placement and size, clear space check, where Sky Blue chips or lower thirds appear, and how the grade pulls mixed-source footage toward the palette. Confirm no Honey Yellow backgrounds and no red anywhere in frame or graphics.

House defaults, unless the script or user says otherwise:

- Logo bottom-left of the live band, **120px wide, around y 1150** — clear of the 600px bottom zone and the 200px right rail, with clear space equal to the bee's head height on all four sides.
- Sky Blue `#C8DDF2` chips behind captions and any lower third; Warm Cream `#FFF7E8` for title cards; Forest Green `#2F5E3B` type on light, Warm Cream type on green.
- Honey Yellow `#F4B400` restricted to a single accent element per reel — an underline, a chip edge, the logo's honey note.
- Grade: lift shadows slightly warm, hold highlights off clinical white, desaturate cold blue cast in window light, and pull any incidental red in frame toward warm brown or replace the asset outright.

### F. VOICEOVER & AUDIO

The voiceover is a woman — the house voice, kept consistent across every reel so the account becomes recognizable by sound alone. Warm, unhurried, conversational; she sounds like someone who has done this before and likes children. Reading a script aloud to one person, not performing to an audience.

Specify: pace in words per minute against the runtime, where she slows or warms, which lines take a smile, and where a breath falls.

**The house voice is locked — do not re-audition it per reel.** Recognisability by sound is the whole point, so treat this as settled unless the user reopens it:

- **Gwen**, ElevenLabs, `voice_id QByd5J8pzbnMMEP2G7eR` — american, middle-aged, warm alto. Chosen by ear 2026-08-17, auditioned on real script lines rather than demo copy.
- Model `eleven_multilingual_v2`; settings stability `0.45`, similarity_boost `0.75`, style `0.0`, speed `0.80`, speaker boost on. Style stays at zero because anything above it adds performance, and this voice is reading to one person rather than performing.
- Audition on the actual script, never on demo copy: a voice can carry generic promo lines and fall apart on the quiet close. Short samples also under-read pace — Gwen measured 145 wpm on three lines and 154 on the full script.
- Render per beat and cut the script's pauses in as measured silence, rather than generating one take and letting the model invent its own timing. `reels/tlh-two-months/render_vo.py` does this and prints a per-beat drift report; reuse it.
- Access is via the ElevenLabs API directly and needs `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` in the environment. Never put the key in the repo or ask for it in chat.

Gwen comes from the shared (community) library rather than the premade set, so check the voice terms before treating her as permanent brand sound. Thousands of voices are reachable there with an API key; the 7-voice default list is not the whole field.

House defaults: **135–150 wpm**, which is the honest ceiling for a 60–75 word script inside 20–35 seconds with room to breathe. She slows on the payoff line and warms into the CTA. No upsell brightness, no sing-song lift at the end of sentences, no clinical flatness, no urgency.

Then: music mood and energy curve, whether room tone stays up, and where the voice sits against the bed. Warm and unobtrusive — music **14–18 dB under the voice**, room tone up at low level so homes sound like homes. No stings, no alarm sounds, no trending audio carrying a mocking or fear-based meme context.

### G. DELIVERY & QC

Export spec, then answer yes/no with one line each. Answer these against the actual cut, not in advance — a checklist filled in from intention rather than inspection is worse than no checklist.

- 1080 × 1920, correct runtime, no letterboxing or blurred pillarbox?
- All text, logo and key action outside the three safe zones?
- Fully legible on mute, cover frame readable at thumbnail size?
- Palette clean — no red, no Honey Yellow background, logo unmodified?
- Mixed stills and motion graded to read as one piece?
- Does every asset model safe practice for this age, background included?
- Does every on-screen child match the age in the script?
- Licences in order — Envato used wherever an identifiable child appears beside a claim, and any Pexels attribution logged?
- Does the voiceover match the house voice?

If any answer is no, fix before delivery.

House export spec: H.264 High profile, 12–16 Mbps VBR 2-pass, AAC 256 kbps 48 kHz, MP4, loudness −14 LUFS integrated with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Burned-in captions across the full runtime, plus a sidecar `.srt`.
